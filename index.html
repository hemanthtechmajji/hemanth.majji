Case study · 01

Reimagining Care@Home

Evolving a remote patient monitoring platform from a developer-built interface into a scalable, user-centered product.

ROLE
Senior UX/UI Designer
DOMAIN
Healthcare · Remote Monitoring
USERS
Clinicians, nurses, care teams
TEAM
PM · UXR · Eng · Design
The story

From developer-led UI to user-centered UX

As Care@Home grew, more patients, more workflows and more clinical requirements were layered onto the platform. The interface, originally built by engineers to get something shipped, became increasingly complex and inconsistent, and it started to show.

My opportunity was to evolve the experience by rebuilding the information architecture, establishing a real design system, and rethinking the clinical workflows that mattered most.

The process

Four moves, in order

  • 01Understand. Mapped the existing product, workflows, and information architecture.
  • 02Listen. Worked with UX research and clinicians to identify usability problems and workflow friction.
  • 03Structure. Improved the IA and established reusable design patterns.
  • 04Solve. Focused on the critical workflows where complexity had the greatest impact.

Starting with IA meant mapping the existing data hierarchy first. What we found was a maze: critical data buried under tabs built from backend logic rather than workflow logic. We reorganized it to follow a nurse's real routine instead: assess, prioritize, act, review.

Design system

Clarity, balanced with compassion

Redesigning components meant balancing clarity with compassion, with colors, spacing and type all chosen to reduce visual fatigue over a long shift. Blue tones signified stability; neutrals let critical alerts stand out naturally instead of alarmingly; typography was tuned for readability at a glance.

Buttons, text fields, tooltips, dropdowns, switches and selectors were rebuilt from scratch as a shared system, and every micro-decision, from filters to toggles and dropdowns, was guided by one principle: predictability and speed. When we tested the new design with nurses, they intuitively completed tasks 40% faster. One clinician summed it up simply: she didn't have to think about where to click; it just worked.

Rethinking the dashboard

A dashboard shaped by clinicians and the market

We reworked the dashboard around the moments clinicians returned to most: identifying patients who needed attention, understanding why, and acting quickly. Their input made the gaps in the existing experience clear. Important signals were difficult to spot, context was spread across the interface, and the path from insight to action took too long.

At the same time, we looked at where remote patient monitoring was heading. As patient panels and care programs grew, the product needed clearer prioritization, scalable workflows, and a stronger foundation for proactive care. The redesign brought those clinical and business needs together, making the dashboard easier to use today while preparing it for what the RPM industry was beginning to demand.

One outcome was the Critical Alerts View, a single-glance summary of high-risk patients shaped by clinician feedback and designed to help care teams act with more confidence.

Results

What changed, measurably

−35%
reduction in clinician task completion time
−25%
decrease in reported usability errors
90%
satisfaction rating among clinicians surveyed

Three things I keep carrying forward: design around the user's real workflow, not the backend's logic. Refine iteratively against real feedback, not assumptions. And use data such as task times, error rates, and satisfaction to know whether a redesign actually worked.

The next step is making the dashboard proactive by surfacing AI-driven insights that suggest interventions before conditions worsen.

Case study · 02

Patient Task Management Module

A remote patient monitoring solution empowering clinicians to manage patient tasks seamlessly instead of waiting on engineers to do it for them.

ROLE
UX/UI Designer
DOMAIN
Remote Patient Monitoring
USERS
Clinicians
IMPACT
80% less backend dependency
The challenge

A system stuck in the past

In the remote patient monitoring system, clinicians had no direct control over patient tasks. Tasks were hardcoded in the backend, requiring manual intervention from engineers for every change. There was no flexibility to modify, add, or track tasks, and no visibility into progress, which caused real delays in patient care.

Key question: how might we enable clinicians to manage tasks independently while improving efficiency and patient engagement?

The solution

A clinician-controlled task module

I designed and implemented a new task management module that let clinicians create and assign tasks to patients directly from their dashboard, modify and track progress without backend dependency, and sync tasks seamlessly with the patient's mobile app. This shifted power from backend-controlled to clinician-driven.

The process

Research, ideation, prototype

  • 01Research. Interviewed clinicians on how they currently handle patient tasks, and audited comparable apps for best practice.
  • 02Ideation. Proposed a calendar-based system, but given dev constraints, settled on a balanced week-view MVP the team could actually ship.
  • 03UI design. Started from the existing calendar patterns; early iterations felt cluttered, so I refined it into a clean, structured layout.
  • 04Prototype & test. Built an interactive prototype, tested with clinicians, and incorporated their feedback before build.
Before / after

The measurable success of UX thinking

BEFORE: BACKEND-ONLY
  • Tasks manually assigned by engineers
  • No ability to modify or customize tasks
  • No visibility into task completion
  • Slow workflow, delaying patient care
AFTER: CLINICIAN-CONTROLLED
  • Self-service task management for clinicians
  • Flexible task creation & updates
  • Real-time tracking of patient progress
  • Instant task assignment & syncing
80%
reduction in backend intervention for task assignments
30%
faster task assignment, improving patient response time
↑
higher clinician engagement and better patient adherence
This shift streamlined workflows, but it also quietly gave power back to the people who needed it most.

Case study · 03

NurseReady

On-demand nurse staffing for hospitals, taken from a raw business idea to a fully designed, development-ready product.

ROLE
UX/UI Designer, 0→1
DOMAIN
Healthcare Staffing
USERS
Hospitals, nurses, admins
STATUS
Design complete, dev-ready
The story

Understanding the idea before designing a single screen

NurseReady was born to close the growing gap between hospitals and qualified nurses, enabling fast, reliable, on-demand staffing through a single platform. It started as a business idea from a client deeply embedded in the hospital ecosystem, who understood the problem well but hadn't yet shaped it into a product.

My first responsibility wasn't to design screens. It was to understand the reality behind the idea. I listened closely to how hospitals actually function during staffing shortages, how nurses take up short-term work, and where trust, payments and coordination usually break down.

Problem statement

Urgency on one side, flexibility on the other

Hospitals frequently face sudden patient inflow, staff unavailability, night shifts, or temporary coverage gaps, while existing hiring systems are built for long-term recruitment and are too slow and rigid for urgent needs.

At the same time, many qualified nurses are willing to take short-duration or flexible shifts, but lack a reliable platform that connects them to nearby hospitals in real time, ensures fair payment, and gives clarity on job details before they accept.

Validating first

Before building anything

I identified the need for a unified system that balances hospital urgency with nurse availability, trust and ease of use. Before committing to execution, I validated it. I studied the market (no direct competitors in India, similar models existed in the US and UK) and spoke directly with hospitals and nurses from our existing network. The response was consistently positive: hospitals valued speed and reliability, nurses liked the flexibility and short-term earning opportunities. That gave us confidence to move fast.

Defining the product

Mapping every workflow before a pixel

I mapped end-to-end workflows for hospitals, nurses and admins to ensure clarity, speed and minimal operational friction. With that clarity, I authored the Product Requirement Document, defining how hospitals raise urgent requests, how nurses receive and accept nearby jobs, how payments flow through the platform, and what happens when a request isn't fulfilled automatically.

From there I designed the end-to-end user flows and reviewed them in detail with designers and developers. Those discussions ensured everyone understood not just what we were building, but why. Only once the logic was solid did we move to visuals.

Key decisions

Designing under real constraints

Timelines were tight, so I made a conscious decision to wireframe only the critical flows first, to validate direction with the client early. Once approved, we moved quickly into UI design, built a component library for consistency and speed, and completed the UI across every platform with interactive prototypes reflecting real-time behaviour.

  • →Real-time, location-based requests to reduce hospital wait time.
  • →Accept-or-decline nurse flow designed for speed, not browsing.
  • →Centralized payments via NurseReady to build trust on both sides.
  • →Admin fallback mechanism to guarantee fulfillment even when nurse response is low.
  • →Clear status visibility across request, assignment, completion and payout.
NurseReady evolved into a fully designed, development-ready staffing system with clear workflows for hospitals, nurses, and platform operations.

Experiment · 04

Chara AI

Redefining remote patient monitoring with intelligent clinical support, a concept exploration for an AI companion embedded in the clinician's daily workflow.

TYPE
Concept / experiment
DOMAIN
Clinical AI assistant
TAGLINE
Your clinical companion
FOCUS
Insights · Action · Better care
The idea

What if the dashboard could think alongside you?

Chara AI is an exploration, not a shipped product. It is a chance to imagine what AI-assisted clinical decision support could look like inside an existing remote patient monitoring workflow, rather than as a separate tool clinicians have to context-switch into. The concept connects clinical guidelines, live patient data, wearable devices, care coordination and population-level analytics through a single, platform-agnostic assistant, surfaced right inside the dashboard a clinician already lives in.

The name draws from Charaka, the father of Indian medicine, a nod to the wisdom and clarity Chara AI aims to bring: intelligence, compassion and efficiency inside modern healthcare.

The problem

Data everywhere, clarity nowhere

Care providers deal with endless data scattered across EHRs, labs and telemedicine tools. They don't need more data, they need clarity. The goal was to design an AI assistant that unifies fragmented medical systems, predicts risk and supports clinicians through natural, intelligent interaction, one that thinks with them, not in place of them.

Primary users: doctors, head nurses and clinical coordinators, all working toward the same three things: a single view of patient health, trustworthy and explainable AI insights, and less time on admin so there's more time for care.

The network

Six sources, one assistant

Clinical guidelines

UpToDate, NICE, and CDC: grounded medical references, not guesses.

Patient data

EHR/EMR, medications, and lab results, already in context.

Wearables & devices

Apple Health, Fitbit, and continuous monitors feeding live signal.

Care coordination

Automated prep, quick literature summaries, referrals, follow-ups.

Patient engagement

Patient-facing app, messaging, and education content.

Analytics & insight

Risk prediction and population health, surfaced proactively.

Design principles

Trust before intelligence

Designing for healthcare AI is less about showing intelligence and more about building trust. The best AI doesn't overwhelm, it assists quietly.

Clean & calm

A clinical visual tone that stays quiet under pressure, not another noisy dashboard.

Context over volume

Context-aware insights instead of raw data dumped on the clinician.

Transparent by design

Every recommendation carries the "why" behind it, not a black-box answer.

Accessible & compliant

Built to WCAG AA, with HIPAA & GDPR compliance as a baseline, not an afterthought.

Capabilities

Where the intelligence shows up

  • →Predictive analytics for early disease detection.
  • →Natural language understanding for clinician queries.
  • →Automated data reconciliation for consistent patient records.
Chara AI is a vision of human-centered intelligence in healthcare, a system that simplifies complexity, supports decisions, and lets care providers do what they do best: care.

Orbito

A lightweight, agency-first work management system designed to bring clarity to everyday work without adding process overhead.

ROLE
UX/UI Designer & Working Partner
CONTEXT
thinkB Creative Agency
USERS
Design & dev teams, management
OUTCOME
Adopted team-wide
Context

Delivery was happening. Clarity wasn't.

I joined thinkB Creative Agency as Senior UX/UI Designer and working partner, responsible for leading the design and development teams. Work was getting delivered, but task ownership, progress, and effort visibility were scattered across conversations and informal methods. Orbito replaces that fragmented workflow of spreadsheets, chat tools and standalone trackers with one shared system.

Observation

Not a performance problem, a systems problem

The challenge wasn't commitment or performance, it was the lack of a shared system: tasks needed repeated clarification, context lived in multiple places, progress wasn't visible at a glance, and managers had limited real-time visibility. My intent was simple: bring clarity to everyday work for both the team and management, without adding overhead or disrupting how people already worked.

The solution

Orbito

I designed and introduced Orbito, a lightweight, agency-first system tailored to our real workflow.

  • →Kanban task flow: To Do → In Progress → Review → Done.
  • →Clear ownership, priority, and timelines per task.
  • →Client → Project → Task structure to preserve context.
  • →Integrated worklogs linked to actual tasks.
  • →Centralized comments & attachments as a single source of truth.
Outcome

Clarity, made the default

Clear task ownership Improved team alignment Fewer follow-ups Visibility without micromanagement Natural team-wide adoption
Orbito is proof that UX thinking applied to operations brings structure and calm, not by enforcing rules but by making clarity the default.
